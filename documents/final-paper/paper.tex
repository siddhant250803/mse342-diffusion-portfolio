\begin{abstract}
We investigate whether a score-based diffusion model for financial return
scenarios can be fine-tuned through KL-penalized stochastic control such that
generated scenarios remain distributionally realistic while becoming more useful
for downstream portfolio optimization. We train a variance-preserving SDE (VP-SDE)
score model on ten S\&P~500 sector ETF daily log-returns from 2014--2020, apply
Gaussian Bures-Wasserstein optimal transport (OT) calibration to align first-order
moments, and fine-tune the reverse diffusion drift with a decision-aligned portfolio
reward evaluated exclusively on 2021 validation data. The fine-tuning objective
balances expected portfolio utility against a Girsanov KL divergence penalty, with
the regularization strength $\eta$ selected on the validation period to prevent
test-period leakage. Over the 2022--2024 test window, the fine-tuned strategy
achieves a Sharpe ratio of \textbf{0.70} versus 0.52 for rolling plug-in Markowitz
and 0.36 for equal-weight, with a reduced maximum drawdown of 14.9\%. A formal
leakage audit confirms zero data-split violations. Bootstrap confidence intervals
(95\%) span approximately 2.1 Sharpe units, reflecting limited statistical power
over a three-year window; results should be interpreted as exploratory evidence of
a realism-performance frontier rather than statistically decisive outperformance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ============================================================

Generative models of financial return distributions offer a principled alternative
to parametric moment estimation for portfolio construction. Rather than fitting a
multivariate Gaussian and applying Markowitz mean-variance optimization (MVO)
\citep{markowitz1952portfolio}, one can sample \emph{scenarios} from a learned
generative model and solve a sample-average stochastic program. This scenario-based
approach can capture non-Gaussian features prominent in financial data, including
fat tails, left skewness, and volatility clustering, that parametric methods routinely miss
\citep{cont2001, mcneil2015quantitative}.

Score-based diffusion models have become the dominant high-dimensional generative
class following \citet{ho2020denoising} and \citet{song2021score}, and their
application to financial data is growing \citep{takahashi2025generation,
aghapour2025solving}. However, a \emph{distributionally realistic} generator is
not automatically a \emph{portfolio-useful} one: the score model may generate
return moments that systematically bias MVO weights toward concentrated,
high-volatility portfolios. Two complementary tools address this gap.
\textbf{Optimal transport (OT) calibration} aligns generated moments to historical
moments post-hoc via the closed-form Bures-Wasserstein affine map
\citep{bures1969extension,brenier1991polar,bhatia2019bures}.
\textbf{Stochastic control fine-tuning} directly reshapes the reverse diffusion
drift to improve a downstream portfolio objective. This second approach is grounded
in KL-penalized optimal control: the KL penalty has a precise path-measure
interpretation via Girsanov's theorem, and the optimal drift correction follows
analytically from the HJB equation, a direct application of the continuous-time
stochastic control theory of \citet{han2024stochastic,fleming2006controlled,
oksendal2003stochastic}.

The central tension is a trade-off between \emph{realism} and \emph{performance}.
The KL penalty weight $\eta$ parameterizes this frontier: smaller $\eta$ permits
larger drift perturbations, while larger $\eta$ keeps the fine-tuned path measure
closer to the base diffusion. Selecting $\eta$ via a held-out validation period
operationalizes the trade-off without touching test data.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) A reproducible end-to-end pipeline with strict train/validation/test splits and
a formal leakage audit; (2) a decision-aligned portfolio reward using validation
returns for gradient signal, replacing an abstract quadratic proxy; (3) honest
separation of Gaussian OT (first-two-moments calibration) from Sinkhorn OT (subset
diagnostic only), avoiding overstatement of OT's corrective power; (4) bootstrap
confidence intervals that transparently reflect the limited power of a three-year
test window.

\section{Mathematical Framework}
\label{sec:math}
% ============================================================

\subsection{VP-SDE Score Model}

Let $X_0 \sim p_\text{data}$ be a $d$-dimensional daily log-return vector. The
variance-preserving forward process \citep{song2021score} is:
\begin{equation}
  dX_t = -\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)\,X_t\,dt + \sqrt{\beta(t)}\,dW_t,
  \quad \beta(t) = \beta_{\min} + t(\beta_{\max}-\beta_{\min}),
  \label{eq:vpsde-forward}
\end{equation}
with $\beta_{\min}=0.1$, $\beta_{\max}=20$. The marginal distribution satisfies
$X_t|X_0 \sim \calN(\alpha_t X_0,\;\sigma_t^2 I_d)$, where
$\alpha_t = e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_0^t \beta(s)ds}$ and $\sigma_t^2 = 1-\alpha_t^2$.
At $t=1$, $X_1\approx\calN(0,I_d)$ serves as the Gaussian prior. A neural network $s_\theta(\bx_t, t)$ is trained via \emph{denoising score matching}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_\text{DSM}(\theta)
  = \E_{t,\,X_0,\,\varepsilon}\!\left[
      \left\| s_\theta(\alpha_t X_0 + \sigma_t\varepsilon,\, t)
              + \varepsilon/\sigma_t \right\|^2
    \right],\quad \varepsilon\sim\calN(0,I_d).
\end{equation}
The target $-\varepsilon/\sigma_t$ is the conditional score
$\nabla_{\bx_t}\log p(\bx_t|X_0)$, which is the denoising score matching identity
of \citet{song2019generative,song2021score}. Scenarios are generated via the
Euler-Maruyama discretization of the reverse-time SDE
\citep{anderson1982reverse,song2021score}, with $t_k$ decreasing from 1 to 0:
\begin{equation}
  \by_{k+1}
  = \by_k + \left[-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t_k)\by_k
          + \beta(t_k)s_\theta(\by_k,t_k)\right]\Delta
        + \sqrt{\beta(t_k)\Delta}\,Z_k,\quad Z_k\sim\calN(0,I_d).
  \label{eq:em-sampler}
\end{equation}
Euler-Maruyama achieves strong order $1/2$ and is the standard baseline sampler
in score-SDE implementations \citep{kloeden1992numerical}.

\subsection{HJB Formulation and KL-Penalized Fine-Tuning}

We add a learnable drift perturbation $\bu_\phi(\by,t)$ to the reverse SDE:
\begin{equation}
  d\by_t = \bigl[-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t) \by_t
    + \beta(t)\, s_\theta(\by_t,t) + \bu_\phi(\by_t,t)\bigr]\,dt
    + \sqrt{\beta(t)}\,dW_t.
  \label{eq:controlled}
\end{equation}
Let $p_\phi$ be the path measure of~\eqref{eq:controlled} and $p_\theta$ the
uncontrolled measure. By Girsanov's theorem \citep{oksendal2003stochastic}, the
KL divergence between the two path measures equals the integrated squared drift
perturbation, weighted by the inverse diffusion coefficient:
\begin{equation}
  \KL(p_\phi \| p_\theta)
  = \E_{\by\sim p_\phi}\!\left[
      \int_0^T \frac{\|\bu_\phi(\by_t,t)\|^2}{2\,\beta(T{-}t)}\,dt
    \right].
  \label{eq:kl-girsanov}
\end{equation}
The KL cost has a precise physical interpretation as the total energy expenditure
of the drift correction integrated along the reverse path.

The fine-tuning objective is:
\begin{equation}
  \max_{\phi}\quad
  \E_{\by_0\sim p_\phi}\!\bigl[R(\by_0)\bigr]
  \;-\; \eta\cdot\KL(p_\phi \| p_\theta),
  \label{eq:finetune-obj}
\end{equation}
where $R:\R^d\to\R$ is a portfolio reward and $\eta>0$ is the KL penalty weight.
Writing $\mathcal{L}^{s_\theta}$ for the infinitesimal generator of the
uncontrolled reverse sampler, the dynamic programming (HJB) equation is:
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t V + \mathcal{L}^{s_\theta}V
  + \sup_{\bu}\left\{\bu^\top\nabla_y V
     - \frac{\eta}{2\beta(t)}\|\bu\|^2\right\}=0,\quad
  V(\by,T)=R(\by).
  \label{eq:hjb-premax}
\end{equation}
The pointwise supremum in~\eqref{eq:hjb-premax} is a standard quadratic maximization.
Its first-order condition yields a closed-form optimal control and the corresponding
HJB equation:
\begin{equation}
  \bu^*(\by,t)=\frac{\beta(t)}{\eta}\nabla_y V(\by,t),
  \qquad
  \partial_t V+\mathcal{L}^{s_\theta}V
  +\frac{\beta(t)}{2\eta}\|\nabla_y V\|^2=0.
  \label{eq:hjb}
\end{equation}
The optimal drift $\bu^*$ is proportional to $\nabla_y V$ and inversely
proportional to $\eta$: large $\eta$ shrinks the perturbation toward zero;
small $\eta$ allows large corrections at the cost of high KL divergence.
In practice $V$ is approximated by a neural control network $u_\phi$ and
optimized by backpropagating through sampled trajectories.

\subsection{Gaussian OT Calibration}

Given empirical moments $(\bmu_\text{gen}, \bSigma_\text{gen})$ from generated
scenarios and target moments $(\bmu_\text{train}, \bSigma_\text{train})$ from the
training set, the Bures-Wasserstein optimal transport map is \citep{bhatia2019bures}:
\begin{equation}
  T^*(\bx)
  = \bmu_\text{train} + A\,(\bx - \bmu_\text{gen}),\quad
  A = \bSigma_\text{gen}^{-1/2}
      \!\left(\bSigma_\text{gen}^{1/2}\bSigma_\text{train}\bSigma_\text{gen}^{1/2}\right)^{\!1/2}
      \bSigma_\text{gen}^{-1/2}.
  \label{eq:gauss-ot}
\end{equation}
This affine map is the unique $W_2$-optimal coupling between the Gaussian
approximations to the generated and training distributions. It directly corrects
the mean and covariance errors that most affect MVO portfolio weights, and is
computed in closed form with a single matrix square root. Non-Gaussian structure
including fat tails, volatility clustering, and higher-order cumulants, is not corrected by
this linear map. For this reason, Sinkhorn OT diagnostics are reported separately
and are not used in the portfolio strategy \citep{peyre2019computational}.

\section{Empirical Design}
\label{sec:design}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Data and Splits}

We use daily log-returns for ten S\&P~500 sector ETFs (XLK, XLF, XLE, XLV, XLI,
XLY, XLP, XLU, XLB, XLRE) via \texttt{yfinance} \citep{aroussi2019yfinance}.
These ten SPDR sector funds span the full S\&P~500 with meaningful but
non-degenerate cross-sector correlations; the $d=10$ dimension is small enough
for full-covariance estimation yet large enough for concentration to be a genuine
concern. The strict three-way split is:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
\textbf{Split} & \textbf{Dates} & \textbf{Trading Days} & \textbf{Use} \\
\midrule
Train      & 2014-01-01 to 2020-12-31 & 1,317 & Score model, scaler, OT target \\
Validation & 2021-01-01 to 2021-12-31 &   252 & Reward eval, $\eta$ selection \\
Test       & 2022-01-01 to 2024-12-31 &   756 & Final evaluation (once) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The training window includes the 2020 Covid drawdown, exposing the score model to
a stress regime. The 2022--2024 test period spans a rising-rate bear market (2022)
and a technology-led recovery (2023--2024), providing a varied out-of-sample
regime. A per-asset standardization scaler is fitted on training data only.

\subsection{Score Model}

A three-hidden-layer MLP score network ($d=10$, hidden size 256, SiLU activations,
sinusoidal time embedding $d_t=64$) is trained with Adam \citep{kingma2015adam}
at learning rate $10^{-3}$, cosine annealing \citep{loshchilov2017sgdr}, 2000
epochs, batch size 256. SiLU activations provide continuous gradients through the
trajectory needed for the subsequent fine-tuning backpropagation step.

\subsection{Portfolio Validation Reward}

For a batch of generated scenarios $Y_0\in\R^{B\times d}$, let
$\hat\bmu = \frac{1}{B}\sum_b Y_0^{(b)}$ and $\hat\bSigma = \mathrm{Cov}(Y_0)$.
The differentiable soft-Markowitz weight vector is:
\begin{equation}
  \hat\bpi = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left((\hat\bSigma + \delta I)^{-1}\hat\bmu\right),
  \quad \delta = 10^{-4},
  \label{eq:soft-mvo}
\end{equation}
a differentiable approximation to the long-only MVO solution. The softmax
replaces the hard long-only constraint, keeps weights positive and summing to one,
and permits gradient flow from the validation reward back through implied portfolio
weights to generated scenario moments. Applied to fixed 2021 validation returns
$r_\text{val}\in\R^{N_\text{val}\times d}$:
\begin{equation}
  R(Y_0) = 252\,\bar r_\text{val}
            - \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\cdot 252\,\mathrm{Var}(r_\text{val})
            - \gamma\,\mathrm{CVaR}_{95}(r_\text{val})
            - \tau\,\mathrm{HHI}(\hat\bpi),
  \label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda=5$, $\gamma=0.5$, $\tau=0.1$, where all portfolio quantities use
weights $\hat\bpi$ applied to validation returns, and
$\mathrm{HHI}(\hat\bpi)=\sum_i \hat\pi_i^2$ is the Herfindahl concentration index.
The four reward terms target distinct objectives: expected return, variance
penalty, explicit CVaR penalty (tail losses that variance misses), and HHI
concentration penalty. Using fixed 2021 validation returns as the reward signal
is the key anti-leakage design: generated scenarios drive implied weights, but
performance is evaluated on held-out data the score model never saw.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\eta$}{eta} Selection Protocol}

The control network $u_\phi$ (tanh-bounded MLP, hidden 128) is trained for 200
epochs per candidate $\eta \in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.50, 1.0, 5.0\}$. The tanh bound
prevents arbitrarily large drift perturbations, consistent with the finite KL-cost
interpretation of~\eqref{eq:kl-girsanov}. The selection criterion is the 2021
\emph{validation} Sharpe ratio; $\eta^*$ is frozen before any test-period
evaluation is run.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Leakage Audit}

All eight leakage checks passed with no failures: train/validation/test dates do
not overlap; scaling, score-model training, and OT calibration use only 2014--2020
data; $\eta$ selection uses only the 2021 validation Sharpe; and NaN/Inf rates are
below 1\%. The audit certifies that the 2022--2024 test backtest was not used to
tune any modeling choice; it does not guarantee that the strategy will generalize.

\subsection{Base Scenario Quality}

Figure~\ref{fig:stylized} compares generated and historical returns across stylized
facts. The score model successfully learns the cross-sectional correlation structure
of the ten sector ETFs, which is the most data-rich signal available at this
dimensionality. However, it underestimates excess kurtosis in every asset: the
generated marginals are near-Gaussian while historical daily returns have fat tails
with kurtosis above 5 in several sectors. The autocorrelation of $|r_t|$ (a proxy
for volatility clustering) is flat for generated paths but significantly positive
for historical returns, confirming that the model does not capture GARCH-type
dynamics. These gaps are expected of a score model trained on i.i.d.\ return
vectors: the model sees each cross-section independently and cannot learn temporal
dependence from the training objective. They motivate the OT calibration (which
partially corrects covariance) and the fine-tuning step (which corrects implied
portfolio weights toward more useful allocations).

\subsection{OT Calibration}

Gaussian OT substantially corrects the moment mismatches introduced by the score
model. Table~\ref{tab:ot-diag} shows the Bures-Wasserstein distance to training
data is reduced by two orders of magnitude (0.0303 to 0.0003) after applying the
affine map $A$ from~\eqref{eq:gauss-ot}. The implied MVO concentration (HHI)
falls from 0.629 to 0.260, reflecting that the uncalibrated score model inflates
per-asset volatilities and distorts the covariance structure enough to produce
very concentrated portfolios. Figure~\ref{fig:ot} shows the per-asset volatility
and correlation effects of calibration in detail. Importantly, the Sinkhorn OT
distance remains at 0.0205, much larger than the Gaussian OT residual, because
it is sensitive to higher-order distributional differences (tail shape, skewness)
that the affine map cannot correct. Gaussian OT is therefore a first-two-moments
fix, not a full distributional match: it cannot substitute for fine-tuning, which
directly optimizes a CVaR-inclusive reward evaluated on actual validation returns.
The two tools are complementary: OT corrects moment-level misspecification, while
KL-penalized fine-tuning adjusts the distribution toward portfolio-useful outcomes
that moment matching alone cannot reach.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\eta$}{eta} Selection (Validation Only)}

Table~\ref{tab:eta} and Figure~\ref{fig:eta} report validation metrics for each
$\eta$ candidate. The Sharpe ratio traces a clear unimodal curve: at $\eta=0.05$
(strong perturbation) the training fails to converge, at $\eta=0.10$ the control
over-steers the distribution toward out-of-distribution scenarios that reduce
both concentration and return quality, and at $\eta=1.0$ and $5.0$ the
regularization is too strong to meaningfully steer the drift. The selected
$\eta^* = 0.5$ achieves validation Sharpe 2.30 and the best combination of
return (34.93\%), moderate tail risk (CVaR 0.0203), and portfolio diversity (HHI
0.316). This value is frozen before any test-period evaluation.

\subsection{Fixed-Scenario Backtest (Test 2022--2024)}

Table~\ref{tab:fixed} and Figure~\ref{fig:final-fixed} report final test metrics.
The fine-tuned strategy achieves the highest Sharpe ratio (0.70), the highest
annualized return (11.31\%), and the lowest maximum drawdown (14.88\%) of all
evaluated strategies. The improvement over Diffusion-Base is particularly notable:
fine-tuning reduces the maximum drawdown from $-30.7\%$ to $-14.9\%$ while
simultaneously increasing return from 8.69\% to 11.31\%. This suggests that the
KL-penalized control corrects the score model's tendency to generate scenarios that
imply overly concentrated, high-variance portfolios, exactly the pathology
identified in the OT calibration diagnostics. Gaussian OT alone is insufficient:
Diffusion+GaussOT achieves Sharpe only 0.390 and still draws down to $-23.0\%$
because it corrects moments but not portfolio-level decision quality. The
Wasserstein-Robust baseline, which applies distributional robustness to MVO without
generative fine-tuning, also fails to close the gap, achieving Sharpe 0.380.

% ============================================================
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{Realism-performance trade-off.}
The central finding is that KL-penalized fine-tuning instantiates a principled,
analytically grounded realism-performance frontier. At $\eta^*=0.5$ (selected on
validation), the fine-tuned model achieves the best risk-adjusted performance in
the fixed-scenario setting. The Girsanov KL cost interpretation is essential here:
the penalty is not a heuristic regularizer but a precise measure of how far the
fine-tuned path measure has been pushed from the base model, in units of squared
drift integrated along reverse-time trajectories. Selecting $\eta$ is therefore
equivalent to choosing a point on the efficient frontier between distributional
fidelity and portfolio utility, a stochastic control analogue of the classical
mean-variance trade-off.

\paragraph{Why rolling mode collapses.}
In the fixed-scenario setting, the model's knowledge of the full 2014--2020
training distribution persists across the test period: scenarios are generated once
using the trained score model and the fine-tuned control, and these same scenarios
are used for all rebalancing decisions. In the rolling setting, the rolling OT
recalibration overwrites this structural knowledge with the most recent 252-day
history at every step, making the diffusion model behaviorally equivalent to
plug-in MVO regardless of fine-tuning. This collapse reveals that the value
proposition of diffusion scenario generation is concentrated in the persistent,
unconditional knowledge of the return distribution, not in the marginal
distributional correction that OT provides at each step. Preserving that value
in an adaptive setting requires regime-conditioned or factor-conditioned generation,
where the model can update its distribution without discarding structural information.

\paragraph{Statistical caution.}
A three-year test window provides approximately 756 daily observations. At this
sample size, 95\% bootstrap Sharpe CI width is approximately 2.1 Sharpe units
(Table~\ref{tab:fixed}). No pairwise difference achieves individual significance
at the 5\% level. This does not mean the results are uninformative: the ranking
is consistent across all seven strategies, the fine-tuned strategy dominates on
three independent metrics (return, Sharpe, drawdown), and the mechanisms driving
the improvement are theoretically motivated. But the results are correctly
characterized as exploratory evidence of a realism-performance frontier, consistent
with prior cautions about backtest power in this horizon \citep{bailey2014pseudo}.

% ============================================================
\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}
% ============================================================

\textbf{Score model expressiveness.} The MLP score network may not capture
multi-modal market regimes or high-order tail dependencies. Transformer-based score
functions \citep{dhariwal2021diffusion} or flow matching \citep{lipman2023flow}
may improve distributional realism and reduce the gap visible in the stylized-fact
diagnostic.

\textbf{Gaussian OT scope.} The Bures-Wasserstein map corrects only first and
second moments. Full Sinkhorn OT \citep{peyre2019computational} or neural OT
\citep{bunne2022supervised} would address higher-order structure at greater
computational cost. The large Sinkhorn residual (0.0205 vs.\ 0.0003 for Gaussian
OT) quantifies how much correctable non-Gaussianity remains after calibration.

\textbf{Differentiable reward.} The soft-Markowitz approximation~\eqref{eq:soft-mvo}
replaces constrained MVO with a softmax projection. Differentiable convex
programming layers \citep{agrawal2019differentiable} would give exact long-only
weights while preserving gradient flow, potentially improving the quality of the
gradient signal and reducing bias in the implied portfolio weights.

\textbf{Bootstrap CI.} Simple i.i.d.\ bootstrap underestimates CI width for
autocorrelated daily returns; block bootstrap \citep{politis1994stationary} would
give more conservative, appropriate intervals. The current CIs should be read as
lower bounds on uncertainty.

\textbf{Scalability.} At ten assets, the full pipeline runs in under 30 minutes
on Apple Silicon. Scaling to hundreds of assets would require mini-batch Sinkhorn
\citep{fatras2021unbalanced}, factored covariance approximations, and more
expressive score architectures to avoid the curse of dimensionality in the
cross-sectional covariance.

% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ============================================================

We have shown that KL-penalized reverse diffusion fine-tuning can shape a
score-based generative model toward portfolio-useful scenarios without completely
sacrificing distributional realism. In the fixed-scenario backtest over 2022--2024,
the fine-tuned strategy with validation-selected $\eta^*=0.5$ achieves Sharpe 0.70,
annualized return 11.31\%, and maximum drawdown 14.88\%, compared to 0.515 Sharpe
and 17.09\% drawdown for rolling Markowitz.

Three methodological controls distinguish this work from an exploratory baseline:
(1) a strict three-way split with a formal leakage audit (zero failures);
(2) honest separation of Gaussian OT (first-two-moments only) from Sinkhorn OT
(subset diagnostic);
(3) wide bootstrap confidence intervals reported without overclaiming significance.

The rolling backtest result reveals that the value of diffusion scenario generation
depends on the persistence of structural knowledge beyond the rolling window.
Future work on regime-conditioned or factor-conditioned generative models could
preserve structural knowledge while allowing adaptation, potentially recovering
the performance advantage in the rolling setting and making the approach
practically competitive with dynamic rebalancing strategies.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{app:repro}

The pipeline is reproducible as a fixed sequence of stages: data download and
splitting, leakage audit, score-model training, scenario generation, OT
calibration, validation-only \(\eta\) selection, one-shot test evaluation, and
figure/table generation. The production run uses 2,000 score-model training
epochs and 10,000 generated scenarios; a reduced smoke-test setting is available
for checking the full workflow quickly. Random seeds are fixed at 42 for data and
score-model training and 0 for fine-tuning.

\section{Leakage Audit Details}
\label{app:audit}

The leakage audit checks the following conditions and passed all of them in the
reported run:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Date ranges of train, validation, and test windows do not overlap.
  \item The scaler is fitted on training data only.
  \item The score model is trained on 2014--2020 data with no test-period reference.
  \item Gaussian OT target moments are computed from training data only.
  \item Sinkhorn OT labeled as subset-only; test period not referenced.
  \item Validation Sharpe is the only criterion used for \(\eta\) selection.
  \item Final metrics are generated only after the validation-selected \(\eta\)
        has been frozen, so no test-period result is used for model selection.
  \item Scenario NaN and Inf fractions below 0.01.
\end{enumerate}

The observed result for the report run is pass: zero failures and zero warnings.
